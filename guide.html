A scan, start to finish

What the operator sees in Doppelmaker Studio at each step of a session, what to do, what to say to the person being scanned, and what the messages mean.

The screens here come from Doppelmaker's digital twin: Studio running for real, with two simulated phones and a simulated person. Your room and your phones will look different; the steps are the same.

The rig page

With the person on a turntable and the phones on stands, you run the session from a chair at the Mac. Studio's Fixed rig page is laid out for that:

  • Left: what each phone sees, live, about twice a second.
  • Right: the current step. What to do, what to say to the subject, and the one button that does it. The space bar presses that button, so you can keep your eyes on them.
  • Top: the steps. Each turns green when it's done, and the page moves on by itself. Click any step to go back to it.

Tick Speak instructions aloud and the Mac reads the "Say to the subject" lines out loud, and says when each turn is done during the scan. Every step also has a folded Why if you want the reasoning.

Connect the phones

On each iPhone, open Doppelmaker and turn on Settings › Rig mode. The phones appear under Phones on the rig page. Give the one in front of the subject role A and the one at their side role B. Identify flashes a phone's screen if you're not sure which is which.

Calibrate

Hold the castle where the subject's chest will be, in view of both phones, and press Take snapshot. Then move it somewhere new and take another: about six to eight in all, at different heights, distances and angles.

What the live views show while you calibrate:

  • Coloured outlines on every marker a phone can read.
  • The castle's frame, hub to each arm, once the phone has worked out exactly where the castle is. The label turns green: "castle tracked". If it says "partly in view", part of the castle is off the edge of the picture; that still works.
  • A 3 × 3 grid. Green squares are where the castle has already been; the yellow "next: here" box is the emptiest part of the picture. Aim for the box in both views at once, as near as you can.

When the error is under 2 mm with at least four snapshots, the step turns green and the page moves on. From then on, don't touch the phones or their stands until you're done scanning. If one gets bumped, the page notices (see Messages).

Record the empty room

Clear the turntable and both pictures: nobody standing behind, no pets. Lights as they'll be for the scan. Press Record empty room; it takes three seconds and stops by itself.

This is the background the subject's outline is cut against. Skipping it still works, but measurements come out about 3–4 mm worse around the body.

Get the subject ready

The card has a short checklist (hair tied up, close-fitting clothes, arms a hand's width from their sides) and the words to say as they step on.

Scan

Press Start scan. Both phones start together after a short countdown. Turn the turntable steadily, twice, at anything from about 20 to 60 seconds a turn, and press Stop (or the space bar) when the subject faces phone A again.

Then the phones send their recordings to the Mac. The subject can relax; this takes a few seconds to a minute on good Wi-Fi.

Arms up and finish

An optional second take with arms raised comes next; skip it if you don't need it. Then Finish: the card says they can step off, and Process scan sends everything to Studio.

Check the result

Processing takes a few minutes. Studio opens on Scan check: a verdict at the top, then one line per thing that was checked, each green, amber or red with a number. It tells you while the subject is still there whether the scan is good, so you can scan again on the spot.

Outline diagnostics shows how the outlines from each phone went, frame by frame: moments when something walked behind the subject, where the fit is loose, and the measurements from the outlines alone.

The next tabs turn the scan into a form:

  • Shape: the surface in its body frame, and a symmetric version if you want one.
  • Cuts: where the form starts and ends. Drag the shoulder handles, or set the neck, shoulders, arm pieces and hem with the controls. The form updates a moment later.
  • Print plan: the form cut into pieces for your printer, each with its core and shell, and how much support it needs.
  • Export: a Bambu Studio project, the STL files, or a complete archive of the visit.

Messages

Problems appear in a Needs attention box under the step card, each with what to do. The ones you're most likely to see:

MessageWhat to do
Phone A is offline.Unlock it, open Doppelmaker, Settings › Rig mode. Both phones must be on the same Wi-Fi as the Mac.
Phone B is hot.Take it out of its case, keep it on its charger, and give it a few minutes. Hot phones slow down and can stop recording.
Phone A is at 15%.Plug it in. LiDAR recording drains the battery quickly.
Phone B has 2.1 GB free.A scan needs about a gigabyte. Free some space on the phone.
Both phones need to track the castle.Move the castle so both views show "castle tracked", or "partly in view".
Snapshot s04 disagrees with the others.The castle probably moved while that snapshot was taken. Open Calibration details and press Drop next to it.
No empty-room recording.Record it before the subject steps on: it's worth 3–4 mm around the body.
A phone is taking a long time to send its recording.Keep Doppelmaker open on both phones; the Wi-Fi may be slow. Discard the take only if a phone was closed.
Recalibrate: phone A moved.Its picture shifted against the room since calibration. Tighten its stand, press Reset calibration, and take new snapshots.
